Every WhatsApp automation platform promises the same thing: "an AI chatbot for your business." And honestly, most of them can answer "what are your opening hours?" without breaking a sweat.

The problem is that basic FAQ answers are the floor, not the ceiling. The moment a customer asks something slightly unexpected, wants to book an appointment with a specific staff member, or asks a question in their second language, the differences between platforms stop being subtle and start being expensive.

So before you commit to any WhatsApp chatbot builder — including ours — here's the evaluation framework we'd use if we were shopping today. Use it on every vendor on your shortlist. Then see how each one measures up against what waai ships as standard.

## TL;DR

When comparing WhatsApp chatbot platforms, judge them on seven things:

1. **Builder depth** — can it handle real logic, or only simple keyword replies?
2. **Hybrid intelligence** — rule-based matching first, AI as the fallback (not the only tool).
3. **Booking** — multi-branch, multi-staff, multi-service scheduling with real calendar sync.
4. **Integrations** — Google Workspace, CRM, broadcast tools, or walled garden?
5. **AI model choice** — locked to one provider, or can you bring your own?
6. **Data ownership and pricing** — who owns the data, and is pricing flat and predictable?
7. **Reseller program** — can you build a business on top of it, or is it consumer-only?

Meta's free in-app Business Agent is a genuinely fine starting point for simple FAQ answering. But it does none of the seven things above at any meaningful depth. Platforms like waai exist precisely because real businesses need more.

## 1. Builder depth: keyword matching vs. real logic

The single biggest differentiator between chatbot platforms is what you can actually *build*.

At the shallow end, you get rule-based responders: match a keyword, send a canned reply. That works until a customer types "can I move my tuesday appointment to later" and the bot stalls.

One tier up, you get visual flow builders with branching, conditions, variables, and loops — the ability to say "if the customer has booked before, skip the intro; if they mention deposits, show payment options." This is where automation starts replacing real admin work instead of just parroting answers.

waai sits deliberately at the deeper end: a no-code visual logic-flow builder with **30+ step types**, including conditions, variables, branching, and loops. You don't need a developer to build flows that behave like a good receptionist.

**What to ask every vendor:** Can I see a demo of a flow with conditional branching and variables — built live, not from a template?

## 2. Hybrid AI: rules first, LLM second

Here's a truth the AI-hype vendors won't tell you: pure LLM chatbots are unpredictable, and pure rule-based bots are brittle. The best architectures use both.

waai's approach: **fuzzy rule-based matching handles known intents first** (fast, deterministic, cheap), and an **LLM handles the long tail** of unexpected questions as a fallback. Your bot is reliable where it matters and smart where it needs to be.

## 3. Booking: the feature that separates toys from tools

If your business takes appointments, this is where most chatbot platforms quietly fall apart. "Booking integration" often means little more than "we'll collect a name and phone number."

What real booking automation looks like:

- **Multi-branch support** — different locations, different availability
- **Multi-staff and multi-service** — per-staff calendars and per-service durations
- **Holidays and working hours** — real availability logic, not double-bookings
- **Deposits and PDF receipts** — money handled properly
- **Automated reminders** — no-shows reduced without manual chasing
- **Two-way Google Calendar sync** — not one-way exports that break when you edit your calendar

waai ships all of this as a standard multi-branch booking system, including two-way Google Calendar sync and PDF receipts.

**Ask every vendor:** Show me a booking flow with two staff members, two services, a holiday closure, and a deposit requirement. If they hesitate, you have your answer.
