There is a fascinating shift happening in how experts view the future of buying and selling. A recent study commissioned by [Meta and authored by the IDC](https://whatsappbusiness.com/blog/agentic-commerce-ai-strategy/) suggests that by 2029, nearly two-thirds of customer interactions will flow through AI agents and answer engines rather than traditional brand websites. This isn't just a trend line; it is a validation of what we have been building at waai. It turns out that the battle for the future of commerce isn't just about having the smartest AI model—it is about having the richest context in the channel where customers actually live.

## TL;DR

The IDC research argues that while standalone LLMs offer convenience, they lack business context, and while website bots offer data control, they lack reach. Messaging platforms sit in the "sweet spot," offering both daily user convenience and the persistent history needed for AI to act intelligently. At waai, we are already operationalizing this for SMBs and agencies by combining the reach of WhatsApp with the logic, booking systems, and CRM depth required to turn these predictions into reality today.

## The Context Gap in Agentic Commerce

The core problem the research identifies is the trade-off between convenience and context. General purpose AI assistants are great for users because they are fast and easy, but they are transactionally "blind"—they don't remember the user for the brand, and they don't share the data back. The brand gets a sale, maybe, but learns nothing from the interaction. On the flip side, building a "concierge" agent on your own website gives you total data ownership, but as the study points out, it requires a customer to have enough intent to leave their daily routine and visit you. That is a high friction point.

> "Context... is the fuller real-time picture an AI agent needs to act well."
>
> — IDC, via WhatsApp for Business

This is why messaging is emerging as the critical infrastructure. It is not just a chat window; it is a persistent thread that lives in the customer's pocket. It captures the "who," the "when," and the "why" in a way that a fleeting search query or an isolated website session simply cannot. For an AI to be truly "agentic"—meaning it can act on your behalf—it needs that history. It needs to know that the customer asking about a reschedule is the same person who had an issue last week, without forcing them to explain it all over again.

## The Behavioral Signal of a Message

One of the most compelling points in the article is the idea that messaging captures behavioral signals that other channels miss. When a customer messages a business, it is often a moment of high intent or immediate need. They might be walking down the street, looking at a product, or trying to resolve a time-sensitive issue.

By the time a customer reaches out to a brand on WhatsApp, they have already bypassed the friction of leaving their preferred app. The businesses that win in this era are the ones that can respect that immediacy with an instant, intelligent response that feels personal. That requires AI that is grounded in the specific context of that business—its services, its tone, and its operational reality.

## FAQ

**Does this mean websites are becoming obsolete?**
No. Websites remain essential for deep content, portfolio showcases, and trust-building. However, as the research suggests, they are becoming less of the starting point for discovery and interaction. The initial "handshake" and much of the ongoing relationship will increasingly happen in messaging threads.

**What is the difference between an LLM assistant and a business messaging agent?**
An LLM assistant (like a general chatbot) is a broad tool that helps users research, but it keeps the data siloed.
