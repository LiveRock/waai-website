## The one rule that shapes everything: the 24-hour window

WhatsApp's messaging model is built around conversation, not blasts. Here's how it works:

- **Inside the 24-hour window** — when a customer has messaged you within the last 24 hours — you can send free-form messages: text, images, anything. This is the open conversation zone.
- **Outside the window** — the customer hasn't spoken to you in over 24 hours — you can only reach out using a pre-approved **message template**.

Message templates are message skeletons you submit to Meta for approval: appointment reminders, order updates, promotional offers, follow-ups. Once approved, you fill in the variable slots (a name, a date, an order number) and send. This is the mechanism that powers every legitimate WhatsApp broadcast.

The practical takeaway: don't fight the window, design around it. Your outbound campaigns will live in templates; your replies and follow-ups can flow naturally. Platforms like waai handle template management and delivery for you, so you're not wrangling approvals through the API manually.

## Build a contact list you're allowed to message

Before your first campaign, get the fundamentals right:

**Opt-in first.** Every contact on your broadcast list should have explicitly agreed to receive messages from you on WhatsApp — via a signup form, a checkbox at checkout, a QR code in your shop, or a WhatsApp opt-in flow. WhatsApp's policy is strict here, and users can report or block you with one tap.

**Make opting out easy.** Include a simple way to stop receiving messages, and honor it immediately. A smaller, willing list will always outperform a big, resentful one.

**Store more than phone numbers.** A phone number alone can't power good campaigns. You want names, preferences, booking history, language, and tags. This is why a CRM isn't optional — it's the foundation of every decent broadcast.

## Segment before you send

The fastest way to get blocked on WhatsApp is sending the same promotional message to everyone. The fastest way to get results is sending the right message to a well-defined group. Practical segments that work:

- **New leads** — a welcome sequence or first-visit offer
- **Active customers** — loyalty perks, cross-sells based on what they've bought or booked
- **Lapsing customers** — a "we miss you" message with a reason to come back
- **Upcoming bookings** — reminders, prep instructions, add-on offers
- **VIPs** — early access, exclusive deals
- **By language** — with waai's multilingual support across 5 locales, you can speak to customers in their language, not just yours

Segmentation isn't just about relevance — it directly protects your sender reputation. Higher engagement and fewer blocks mean Meta trusts your number more, which keeps your templates approved and deliverable.

## Write templates that don't feel like templates

Because outbound messages require templates, your copy has to work harder. A few rules we've seen hold up across industries:

**Lead with value, not with "Dear customer."** Use variables. "Hi {{name}}, your appointment at {{branch}} is tomorrow at {{time}} — reply to reschedule" beats a generic blast every time.

**Keep it short and give one clear action.** WhatsApp messages are conversations. One message, one purpose: confirm, book, claim, reply.

**Respect frequency.** WhatsApp is personal space. A few high-value messages per month beats daily noise. If you wouldn't want the message as a recipient, don't send it.

**Match template type to purpose.** Utility templates (reminders, confirmations) are easy to approve and always welcome. Marketing templates (offers, announcements) are where relevance and restraint matter most.

## FAQ

### Can I send unlimited broadcasts on WhatsApp?

There's no fixed campaign limit, but Meta applies messaging quality rules and tier-based volume limits to your number. Messaging people who want to hear from you — with relevant, opted-in content — is what keeps your limits healthy and your number in good standing.

### Do my customers need to save my number to receive a broadcast?

No. On the WhatsApp Business API (which waai is built on), your messages reach customers directly — they don't need to have saved your contact. They do need to have opted in.

### What exactly is the 24-hour window?

It's Meta's rule that you can send free-form messages only within 24 hours of a customer's last message to you. Outside that window, business-initiated messages must use pre-approved templates. waai manages this for you, so you never accidentally send a message that violates the window.

### How do I get message templates approved?

You submit your template to Meta with a clear purpose and category (utility or marketing), and it's reviewed for quality and policy compliance. Keep them honest, specific, and value-driven and approval is straightforward. waai handles template management in-platform so you don't have to fight the API.

### How is this different from a WhatsApp group or status update?

Groups and statuses are one-to-many social features, not business messaging. Broadcasts via the API deliver an individual, private message to each contact — no group chaos, no needing to be saved, with delivery and engagement you can actually track.
